
# A problem analysed from Geospatial

## Collect

Collects more meaningful data to address the challenge

- What needs to be collected (Qualitative V Quantitative, Primary V Secondary)
- How the data is acquired from the source (Hourly, Daily Weekly)
- How will it be collected (Surveys, FDGs, Crowdsourcing, Sensors, Fieldwork)
- Why is the data collected (Planning Purposes)
## Combine

Combines multiple data sources for more consistent , accurate & useful Information than any individual data source

- How the data can be combined , fused, converted
- How the data can be transformed
- What data needs to be aggregated/ re-classified? (What Scale? What categories?)
- How different data can be fused together? (a common identifier? Spatial reference?)
## Collaborate

Collaborating to achieve more with limited resources, **sharing is caring**.

- Where the data is stored for sharing and Distribution (Available GeoPortals)
- How the data can be managed and accessed by others
- What format should the data be in? (Shapefiles, Spreadsheets, Images)
- How data can be managed and accessed by others? (Data access framework, Data Security)

## Consume

Consuming data to make informed decisions by seeing & understanding patterns

- How the value in data can be unlocked
- How the data can be visualised, analysed and modelled:
	-  (Bar charts, maps, graphs, infographics, word cloud, timeline)
- What analysis can be performed on the data(Modelling, Predicting, Correlation, Trends)


# Why is the GDLC (GeoData Life Cycle) Important

 The Lifecycle is able to harness spatial data and technology to address a wide range of societal, environmental, and economic challenges.

- Informed Decision Making
- Resource Management
- Environmental Monitoring and Management
- Disaster Management and Mitigation
- Urban Planning and Development
- Public Health and Safety
- Precision Agriculture
- Infrastructure Development
- Climate Change Adaptation
- Scientific Research

IREDUPPICS

## Informed-Decision Making
The GDLC application cycle helps decision-makers across different sectors make more informed decisions  by providing them with accurate, up-to-date, and spatially-relevant information.

## Resource Management
Through the GDLC application cycle, resources such as water , land, and energy can be efficiently managed and allocated based on spatial analysis and modelling.

## Environmental Monitoring and Management
Geospatial applications facilitates monitoring and management of environmental resources and ecosystems, enabling proactive measures for conservation, pollution control, and sustainable development.

## Disaster Management and Mitigation
Geospatial Technologies play a big role in disaster preparedness, response, and recovery efforts by providing real-time situational awareness, risk assessment , and damage assessment.

## Urban Planning and Development
The GDLC application cycle •supports urban planners and policymakers in designing sustainable and resilient cities by analysing spatial data related to infrastructure, transportation, land use, and demographics.

## Public Health and Safety
Geospatial applications contribute to public health and safety initiatives by mapping disease outbreaks, identifying healthcare facility locations, optimizing emergency response routes, and assessing environmental health risks.

## Precision Agriculture 
In agriculture, the geospatial application cycle helps farmers optimize crop yields, minimize resource use, and manage field variability through precision agriculture techniques such as soil mapping, crop monitoring, and variable rate application.

## Infrastructure Development
Geospatial data and analysis inform infrastructure development projects by assessing terrain suitability, identifying optimal routes for roads and pipelines, and assessing environmental impacts.

## Climate Change Adaption
Geospatial applications assist in monitoring and modelling the impacts of climate change, facilitating adaptation strategies, and identifying vulnerable regions and communities.

## Scientific Research
Geospatial technologies support scientific research across disciplines such as ecology, geology, climatology, and archaeology by providing spatial data visualization, analysis, and modelling capabilities.



# TLDR
The geospatial application cycle enables deeper insight into spatial relationships, patterns, and trends—supporting smarter decisions and promoting sustainable development across diverse fields.
